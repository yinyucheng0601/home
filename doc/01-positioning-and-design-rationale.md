> **Core proposition:** A designer who gives structure to complexity.

> **Desired impression:** Calm, precise, elegant, technically deep.

## 1. Why this website exists

The website's primary job is to **showcase professional credibility to multiple audiences**, rather than promote a specific visual technique, demonstrate an isolated AI visualization, or serve merely as a directory of repositories. It should communicate professional judgment in seconds and substantiate that judgment through carefully chosen work, functioning artifacts, and meaningful details.

**Primary outcome:** Visitors perceive someone rare: **a designer who makes complex systems elegant, precise, and understandable.**

The site must work even if visitors never open a technical demo. It should also reward deeper exploration by designers and engineers who want evidence of rigor and implementation ability.

### What the website is not

- Not a conventional “AI Infra UX Designer portfolio” template.
- Not an AI dashboard disguised as a portfolio.
- Not a 3D visual-experiment homepage dominated by a generic layer stack.
- Not an interface whose complexity requires the visitor to learn a technical vocabulary before understanding the person.
- Not a collection of beautiful effects without clear information hierarchy or proof of work.

## 2. Audience model and success criteria

| Audience | Initial question | Feeling to create | Evidence to offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Non-designers, recruiters, hiring managers, general visitors** | Who is she, what does she do, and why should I trust her? | “She is professional, thoughtful, confident, distinctive, and easy to understand.” | Clear self-introduction, approachable language, recognizable project outcomes, polished and responsive experience. |
| **Designers and design leaders** | Does her visual judgment have depth and consistent rules? | **“I trust her taste, precision, and depth.”** | Typography, spatial hierarchy, calibrated spacing, meticulous micro-interactions, coherent information system, careful restraint. |
| **Engineers, researchers, AI infrastructure collaborators** | Is this designer technically serious, or only presenting compelling visuals? | **“She can think with systems.”** | Technically meaningful diagrams, executable demos, transparent role/scope, code and GitHub links, consistent semantics, functioning interfaces. |

**Cross-audience design criterion:** The same detail should be accessible as visual quality to a general audience and legible as technical rigor to an expert. Avoid making expert comprehension a prerequisite for appreciating the site.

### Three types of trust

1. **Taste:** Visual decisions are intentional, restrained, and coherent.
2. **Precision:** Labels, scales, alignment, motion, and information structures are meaningful rather than decorative noise.
3. **Depth:** Work reveals sound systems thinking, genuine problem-solving, and implementation ability.

## 3. Public persona

### Identity hypothesis

**A graceful systems designer.**  
**A designer who gives structure to complexity.**

The phrase “graceful” expresses the manner, not a superficial aesthetic. The site should feel quietly confident: technically informed without being inaccessible, refined without preciousness, serious without heaviness.

### Personality spectrum

| Prefer | Avoid |

| Calm | Aggressive, loud, overanimated |
| Precise | Needlessly dense, fussy, pseudo-technical |
| Elegant | Ornamental for its own sake |
| Technically deep | Performative complexity |

- **“I trust her taste, precision, and depth.”**

## 4. Core narrative: the person comes before the visual experiment

A visitor should understand the following progression:

1. **Who:** A product designer working with complex computational systems, developer tools, and interactive experiences.
2. **How:** Combines systems thinking, visual communication, research, and implementation.
3. **Proof:** Selected work shows complexity made understandable and useful.
4. **Depth:** A curated artifact index contains demos, skills, and visuals for further exploration.
5. **Personality:** Subtle measurement details, elegant material studies, and considerate interaction create character without obscuring content.

The **first screen is an identity field, not a giant hero object**. Selective rendered details can appear as evidence and delight, not as a compulsory spectacle.

## 5. The key creative decision: Measured Editorial Workspace

A **measured, elegant editorial workspace** uses an almost invisible engineering apparatus around lucid content: faint scales, aligned rules, reference marks, section indices, and restrained visual highlights.

Its distinctive metaphor is **measurement**, not a literal measuring tool. It suggests calibration, attention, confidence, and craftsmanship. The user should feel they are moving through a considered personal space, not operating CAD software.

### Four conceptual layers

1. **Quiet Field** — subtle warm-white / soft-gray ground; space to breathe; optional understated grid or baseline cues.
2. **Measurement System** — fine horizontal and vertical rulers, cursor-aligned marks, calibration ticks, figure/reference indices; subordinate to reading.
3. **Editorial Content** — typographic identity, narrative, proof of work, clear navigation and hierarchy.
4. **Refined Objects** — sparse, high-quality renders, optical materials, and small meaningful interactive specimens.

### Why the ruler matters

The top and left cursor-following ruler in [imdaryl.com](https://imdaryl.com/) and the supplied screenshot are compelling not because measuring is inherently relevant to a portfolio, but because they embody **instrument-like precision without visual weight**.

- A non-designer perceives high finish and care.
- A designer perceives disciplined grids, scale, alignment, and responsive interaction.
- An engineer perceives consistency between physical metaphor and underlying behavior.

The ruler should be a **signature detail**, not a decorative clone of another website. Make its behavior appropriate to this site's geometry and information architecture.

## 6. External references: what to borrow, what not to copy

| Reference | Transferable principle | Avoid literal borrowing |
|---|---|---|
| [Paper Mono](https://paper.design/mono) | Systematic typographic rhythm, measurements, character-level care, specimen-like exploration. | Reproducing the type specimen wholesale or making every page visually coded. |
| [Ghost](https://ghost.ai/) | Continuous shifts between physical form, component structure, and topology. | Making abstract exploded 3D structures the global homepage centerpiece. |
| [CO'WATCH](https://focus.itshassco.com/wt) | Spare instrument-like elegance, proportions, understated precision, calm visual tempo. | Building a literal clock aesthetic or overusing dials. |
| [Daryl](https://imdaryl.com/) | Engineering/editorial composition, fine references and indices, cursor-following rulers, surprising polish in minor behaviors. | Reproducing its exact layout, annotations, or interactions unchanged. |
| [AI_Infra repository](https://github.com/yinyucheng0601/AI_Infra) | The user's own technical content, semantic diagramming rigor, interactive artifacts, and systems reasoning. | Turning the personal website into a specialist dashboard or using Layer Stack as a generic homepage symbol. |

The user's work provides **evidence and intellectual structure**; external references provide selected methods of expression. The visual identity must emerge from that synthesis, not a collage.

## 7. What deserves emphasis

### A. Editorial quality first

Typography, information hierarchy, language, spacing, image selection, and project sequencing establish trust before any interactive effect has time to play.

### B. Micro-delight rather than macro-spectacle

The most memorable behaviors should be small and purposeful: a cursor ruler, a softly snapping measurement line, a figure reference appearing on hover, restrained optical highlights, a subtle index update. **One glance: calm. Second glance: surprise.**

### C. Substance for deep readers

The website must demonstrate capabilities, not just suggest them. Case studies should clearly attribute roles, constraints, design rationale, outcomes where publishable, and working artifacts. Demos, skills, and visuals should have concise explanations and direct access.

### D. Graceful contrast

A light editorial foundation may frame dark technical canvases or highly rendered objects. Dark mode is not a requirement for the personal site simply because some AI Infra tools use dark interfaces. Contrast exists to make specific work legible and arresting.

## 8. Content logic

**Selected Works = narrative proof.** A small number of accessible case studies demonstrate high-value problem solving.

**Artifact Index = deeper evidence.** Demos, skills, and visuals provide exploratory access for peers and technically curious visitors.

**About = coherent individual identity.** Connect the transition from architecture to UX, operating systems, and AI infrastructure without turning the page into a résumé dump.

This creates progressive disclosure: casual readers gain a complete picture quickly; expert visitors can dig into code, execution, design methods, and technical semantics.

### Three central capability claims

- **I make complexity legible.**
- **I design systems with taste.**
- **I build interactive artifacts.**

Use these as an internal editorial framework. They need not appear verbatim as three sales slogans.

## 9. Rejected / deprioritized directions

1. **Generic 2.5D Layer Stack hero:** Overfamiliar within AI visualization; overweights a specific technical object before introducing the person.
2. **Fully immersive 3D portfolio:** Raises friction, can feel performative, obscures reading, and introduces avoidable accessibility/performance risks.
3. **Heavy dark dashboard styling:** Communicates operational tooling rather than a light, versatile personal identity.
4. **Three equal top-level tabs for Demo / Skill / Visual:** Too library-like as a primary first impression; better as a deeper artifact taxonomy.
5. **Glass everywhere:** Diminishes refinement, weakens readability, and makes material effects feel generic.
6. **Technical ornament without meaning:** Fake parameters, arbitrary rulers, decorative connection lines, and invented numerical precision erode credibility.

## 10. Quality bar and decision test

Each proposed element should pass these questions:

- **Audience:** What does a first-time visitor learn or feel from this element?
- **Narrative:** Does it clarify the person or their work?
- **Precision:** Is its geometry or information consistent and meaningful?
- **Restraint:** Does it improve the page more than it competes for attention?
- **Depth:** Is there genuine substance beneath it?
- **Performance:** Can it remain elegant on a laptop, phone, and reduced-motion setting?
- **Originality:** Is this an authored choice rather than an imitation of a reference?

A useful rule: **If removing an animation improves comprehension without losing character, simplify it.**
